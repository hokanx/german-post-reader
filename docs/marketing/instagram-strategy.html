Papkram Strategy brief

Instagram: the Arabic-first wedge.

Papkram isn't in the translation niche — it's in bureaucratic anxiety relief. Every direct competitor found is English-only. That gap, plus a calm voice in a loud category, is the whole plan.

22 Aug 2026Niche · Competitors · Positioning · 30 daysSolo-founder execution
Start here

Five decisions

Niche

Bureaucratic anxiety relief, not translation. The job isn't "translate this" — it's "am I in trouble, and by when?" Lead with the deadline, always.

Wedge

Arabic first, English second, Turkish third. Every direct competitor anchors on "plain English." The RTL work is already shipped — that's a moat nobody else has paid for.

Voice

Loud design, quiet words. The playful identity stops the scroll; the calm copy earns the trust. Be the account that lowers the reader's heart rate.

Engine

Two Reels + one carousel per week. "One German word" Reels for reach, "Brief der Woche" carousels for saves. Batched in one Sunday session. Nothing else until something breaks out.

Guardrail

Never legal advice, never a real user's letter, never a machine-translated caption. Each of the three is fatal in a different way.

Evidence note

What's verified, what isn't

Instagram.com is blocked from the environment this research ran in, so every follower count below comes from third-party write-ups, not from the profiles themselves. Market sizing, the direct-competitor list and platform mechanics are solid — they come from official statistics, live product pages and 2026 platform studies. Treat the account-level numbers as leads to verify, not facts. There's a 40-minute phone audit at the end that closes the gap.

01 · The niche

Three markets, one product

They share a product. They do not share a feed, a language, a creator set, or a purchase psychology. The asymmetry is the whole strategy: the segment with the most pain and the least competition is the one every competitor ignores.

Why Arabic wins the first slot

Highest legal jeopardy per letter (Jobcenter, Sozialamt, Ausländerbehörde, BAMF), the strongest sharing behaviour of the three communities, and the thinnest competition — because trustworthy Arabic RTL output is real work, and Papkram has already done it.

Two gaps nobody is filling
  • Nobody leads with Arabic or Turkish. Every single one anchors on "plain English."
  • Nobody offers the reply loop — a German draft plus a translation of that draft, so you know what you're about to send. Harder to copy than the summary.
  • The category is at the many-identical-wrappers stage, which means distribution and trust decide it, not features. Hence Instagram.

Tier 2 — content competitors, i.e. your distribution

They have the audience and no product. You have the product and no audience. These are collab targets, not enemies. Reported sizes, unverified: Simple Germany ~42k (the polished English benchmark), Handbook Germany ~24k (9 languages, institutional, closest to an incumbent in your multilingual lane), ExpatEase ~11k, isteyim.official ~171k (Turkish, jobs). On the Arabic side: germanyarabic.de and arab-deutschland.com — news-and-guides pages with a heavy Facebook and Telegram tilt.

Tier 3 — what people actually do today

Ranked by real usage. Each one is a content angle:

  • Ask a bilingual relative. Free, trusted, slow, embarrassing — and sometimes wrong.
  • Photo → Google Translate or ChatGPT. Gives words, not consequence. Hallucinates the deadline.
  • Post it in a Facebook group. Free, fast, and a privacy disaster. Your single best content angle.
  • Caritas / Diakonie / Migrationsberatung. Free, correct, booked out three weeks.
  • Nothing — until it becomes a Mahnung.
03 · Positioning

The calm second opinion for German post

Papkram reads the letter, tells you plainly what it wants, what happens if you ignore it, and by when — then writes the German reply, with a translation so you know what you're sending.

Unlike translation apps, it gives meaning and a deadline, not words. Unlike the group chat, nobody else sees your letter.

Message hierarchy — always in this order

  • Is this urgent? → deadline extraction. The #1 anxiety, so the #1 hook.
  • What do they actually want? → plain summary, payments, appointments.
  • What do I write back? → German reply + translation. The moat.

Loud design, quiet words

The identity is saturated, chunky, sticker-accented. The voice is calm and clinical. Running both at once is the advantage — this audience is already being shouted at by immigration-lawyer ads and scaremongering news pages. Nobody is occupying calm.

✓ "This letter isn't a fine. It's a request for a document. You have until 14 March."

✗ "⚠️ DON'T IGNORE THIS OR YOU'LL BE DEPORTED 😱"

Never say these — legal, not stylistic

Germany's Rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz makes case-specific legal assessment a licensed activity, and the Smartlaw ruling punished marketing claims like "cheaper and faster than a lawyer." General information published without reference to an individual case stays outside the RDG — that's where all content must live.

  • "legal advice" · "Rechtsberatung" · "استشارة قانونية" · "hukuki danışmanlık"
  • "cheaper than a lawyer" · "instead of a lawyer" · "Anwalt sparen"
  • "we'll object for you" · "we'll fight the Bescheid"
  • Any outcome promise or guarantee — already cut from the offer. Keep it cut.
04 · Accounts

Two now, three later

Instagram's distribution is language-modelled — a trilingual feed gets shown to nobody properly. But three half-fed accounts lose to one well-fed one, so don't open all three.

Now

@papkram + @papkram.ar

Arabic is the wedge. English is the brand, press and partner story — and the EN carousel already exists.

Week ~8

@papkram.tr

Turkish incumbents are stronger. Enter with a format that already works instead of experimenting there.

Two things to do this week, both free
  • Register papkram, papkram.ar, papkram.tr, papkram.app, getpapkram on Instagram and TikTok, defensively. Five minutes.
  • Run a DPMA trademark search (classes 9 and 42). papierkram.de is an established German invoicing SaaS and the names are close. Free check, expensive miss.
05 · Content

Three formats, run until they break

Consistency of format beats consistency of topic. Pillar mix stays fixed: 40% decode a letter, 25% deadline literacy, 20% quiet reassurance, 15% product proof. Never post scare content, deportation-news commentary, or immigration-politics takes — all of it draws the wrong audience and burns the calm position.

A · flagship — 1× per week

"Brief der Woche" carousel

Six slides, same skeleton every time: the letter with a red Frist? sticker → what it actually is → what they want → the deadline, huge → what happens if you ignore it → CTA. The existing card1 design already does slide one.

Synthetic letters only. Generate from the app's own seed letters — never a real user's mail, even blurred, even with permission.

B · the likely breakout — 2× per week

"One German word" Reels, 12–20 seconds

One bureaucratic word per Reel: Frist, Widerspruch, Bescheid, Mahnung, Nachzahlung, Säumniszuschlag, Anhörung, Rückforderung. Word on screen → "if you see this, you have X days" → the sentence it hides inside, in real letter German → done.

Cheap, infinitely repeatable, extremely saveable — and it teaches the exact vocabulary that makes people trust the product.

C · no face needed — 1× per week

"The table"

Overhead shot of a kitchen table. A hand drops an unopened letter. Text overlay carries the internal monologue. Phone comes in, scans, anxiety resolves. Fifteen seconds — the product demo disguised as a feeling.

Cadence per account: 2 Reels + 1 carousel + 3–4 Story frames a week. That's it. Reels carry roughly double the reach of carousels; carousels collect the most saves. Batch the whole week in one three-hour Sunday session.

08 · Loops

Growth

  • Use the loop you already shipped. Poster sharing to Story and WhatsApp is live. Two small changes make it a real loop: put @papkram on the poster image itself, and prefill the share caption with the handle.
  • Collabs, not shoutouts — the fastest path to the first 5,000. Offer free lifetime access plus a co-branded Brief der Woche posted as a Collab to both feeds. Target order: mid-size Arabic and Turkish Germany accounts at 10k–80k, where a collab is a favour rather than an invoice → multilingual info accounts → English creators last, they're the most professionalized and slowest.
  • Comment-to-DM. Pin "Comment BRIEF and I'll send you the free link" on the highest-reach Reel. DM volume is itself a ranking signal. Keep the reply to one sentence and a link — never let a DM become case-specific advice.
  • Mirror everything to TikTok. 23M+ German monthly users, cheaper reach, and both diaspora communities are heavily active there. The vertical video already exists — don't throw the asset away.
The pinned comment — post it first on every letter post

ℹ️ Papkram explains what a letter says — it isn't legal advice and we can't advise on your individual case. For that: Migrationsberatung, Caritas/Diakonie, or a lawyer. Questions about how the app works: DM anytime.

That one comment is your RDG shield, your comment-moderation policy, and — because it points people to free help — a trust signal. It does all three at once.

09 · Metrics

Five numbers, weekly

  • Reach per format — decides next week's mix
  • Saves + shares — the true signal in an information niche; a save means "I'll need this"
  • Profile visits → link taps — target above 8%
  • PostHog signups tagged src=ig — the only number that actually matters. The landing page already reads this param, so it's zero engineering.
  • Follower growth — the vanity one. Check it last.

Kill any format that hasn't produced a top-3 post within three weeks of trying it.

10 · Risks

What could break this

HighRDG exposure via comments and DMs
Pinned comment on every post, no outcome promises, "not legal advice" in all three bios.
HighPrivacy optics
A product that ingests Jobcenter mail asks for enormous trust. Synthetic letters only; make "never used to train AI" a recurring post, not fine print.
HighMachine-translated captions
Native writers or nothing. A translation product with clumsy translations kills itself.
MediumTrademark collision with papierkram.de
DPMA search before any paid spend or print run.
MediumAnti-immigration pile-ons in comments
Keyword filters from day one; restrict comments on politically adjacent posts; never post immigration politics.
MediumCategory commoditizing
Compete on languages, reply drafts and calm voice — not features. Distribution is the moat here.
Before anything else

The 40-minute phone audit

Instagram was unreachable from where this research ran. Do this on your phone and write the answers back into the strategy doc:

  • Search Instagram for briefeasy, amtli, behördenklartext, expat.fyi — and confirm papkram is free. For each: followers, last post, frequency, best format, and whether they post in any language other than English.
  • Search the hashtags #jobcenter, #auslaenderbehoerde, #الحياة_في_ألمانيا, #almanyadayasam. Screenshot the top 9 of each — the format of the top posts is your format answer, free.
  • Open @simplegermany, @handbookgermany, @expat_ease, @isteyim.official. Record followers and best recent post. That's your quality bar and your collab shortlist.
  • Find 10 Arabic and Turkish accounts at 10k–80k posting practical bureaucracy content. That list is your week-2 outreach.

One finding would change the plan: a direct competitor already running an Arabic account with real traction. If the audit turns that up, the wedge gets re-cut.
